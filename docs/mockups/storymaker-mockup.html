Suggestions, roughly in the order I'd build them

Effort is a guess for this codebase: S is an afternoon, M a few days, L a bigger feature. Numbers match the pins on the Studio view.

Storybook: make it an enchantment

The current reader is a flat two-page panel with a select, a slider and "Spread 3 / 7" above it. It reads like a settings screen. The fixes below are mostly CSS in styles.css and a small state machine in Storybook.tsx; the Storybook tab of this mockup shows all of them working.

1

Open with a ritual

Start on the closed book: a gilded cover with the story's title on a dark velvet stage, gold motes drifting. "Open the book" swings the cover back on its spine while the book slides to center. Only the first visit in a session; after that, land on the reader's last page. With reduced motion, a cross-fade.

M
2

Real page turns

A 3D leaf that carries the current right page over onto the left, with a soft shadow passing under it. Click either page edge, swipe, or use the arrow keys you already handle.

M
3

Put the controls away

The chrome fades after a couple of seconds without movement. Skin and size move into one "Aa" popover. "Spread 3 / 7" becomes a row of beads, with a diamond where each chapter starts and its title on hover.

S
4

A fairy-tale face and gilded initials

IM Fell English for the page (OFL, like your other fonts), Cinzel Decorative for titles and a gold-leaf drop cap at each chapter start, a fleuron between scenes, and small-caps folios. Fraunces and Atkinson stay as choices.

S
5

Chapter openers get their own page

Each chapter opens on a full page with its number, title and a small emblem, framed by a hairline border. Text starts on the following page. This is a change to the block rules in storybookPages.ts.

S
6

Make it look like an object

Leather boards that overhang the pages, a gilt fore-edge, a stack of page edges, a shadow in the gutter and a faint paper grain. Later, the stack could get thinner on the right as you read.

S
7

Ink the words in

When a page arrives, its paragraphs come in one after another from a soft blur, like ink settling. It is short, and off under reduced motion.

S
8

Optional sound

A celesta-like chime when the book opens and a paper rustle per turn, synthesized in Web Audio like your room tones so no audio files ship. Off by default.

S
9

Covers from the shelf

Use each book's spine color, and an optional cover picture, for its cover. Skins become Gilded (new default), Illuminated and Glow.

M
10

An ending that invites writing

After the last written page, a closing page: "The story so far ends here", with a button that opens the Hearth at that scene.

S
11

Export the book

Forge Tales' Atelier compiles to .docx, .epub and .pdf. The Storybook is your natural "compile" step: the same pagination and typography, written to EPUB first.

L

Getting around

Forge Tales presents a few clearly named modules. StoryMaker has more rooms than that, in one flat list.

12

Group the rooms in the rail

Write (Hearth, Storybook), World (People, Lore, Bonds, Vision), Time (Threads, Calendar). Same icons, small section labels, and an active state with a gold edge.

S
13

A book switcher in the top bar

The open book's spine and title, with a menu of the shelf. The shelf on Home stays as the place to arrange and add books.

S
14

Ctrl K command palette

Turn Search into a palette: results labeled Person, Lore, Scene or Thread, plus actions like "New scene" or "Light the lamp".

M

Home

The shelf stays as the front door. Everything else on Home sits below it.

15

Keep the shelf, and let it glow

Same bookcase, same rules: one spine per story, New story at the end of the row, × on hover. Add a warm lamp light falling across the wood, gilt bands and gold titles on the spines, a lift on hover, and pull the open book forward with a gold glow. Each spine's leather color becomes that book's Storybook cover (item 9).

S
16

Lead with "Continue writing"

The last scene's title, a line from it, a ring for the word goal, and two buttons: write it or read it. The lamp ritual moves into this card, and stats go to a smaller side card.

S
17

Room previews instead of counts

Faces from People, pictures from Lore, and the next threads on the calendar, so Home doubles as a map of the world.

M

Worldbuilding rooms

18

Character cards with crests and bonds

A portrait, or a monogram crest in the person's color when there is no picture. The role as a chip, and the people they are bonded to, right on the card.

M
19

Threads as a timeline

A horizontal strip across your named months, each event colored by its main person. Click an empty day to start a thread there.

M
20

Hover cards in the manuscript

Names of pinned people and lore in the Hearth get a dotted underline. Hovering shows their card, so you can check eye color without leaving the scene.

M
21

Import from other tools

Forge Tales imports Markdown from Notion, World Anvil, LegendKeeper and Campfire. Your Obsidian importer already turns loose notes into lore; give it presets for those exports' folder layouts.

L
22

Say the privacy promise up front

Forge Tales leads with "No AI · Local & Offline · Private". You can say "Offline · Private · AI only when you ask" on first run and on the shelf.

S

Worth borrowing from Forge Tales

  • Modules with plain names and a clear job
  • Relationship tags on character cards
  • A wiki grid of picture tiles
  • Named theme presets with fonts attached
  • Compile to EPUB, PDF and Word

Already better in StoryMaker

  • The lamp ritual and room tones
  • Obsidian vault round-tripping
  • The Vision canvas with music
  • A local model that only suggests

Where to start

Items 3 to 7 are a small CSS and pagination pass and change the Storybook's feel the most. Items 1 and 2 add the cover and page-turn motion on top.
