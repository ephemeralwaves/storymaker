What changes, and why

Today the calendar is "Tideglass Year 418" with ten invented months, and there is no place to rename the era, change the year, or set weekdays. This rethink moves all of that into one setup page and starts every new story on the Gregorian calendar.

For the writer

1

Gregorian by default

New stories start on January to December, the 7-day week, Gregorian leap years, CE/BCE, and today's date as "now". Nothing fantasy-flavored until you choose it.

2

Presets as starting points

Gregorian (the default), Gregorian with your own names, the Thai solar calendar (same months and leap years, Thai names, years in the Buddhist Era: 2026 CE is 2569 BE), and Blank. A preset only replaces the structure. Threads keep their dates.

3

"Now in the story"

One date that the Calendar opens on and that Home, the Storybook title page and the AI context all use. It replaces the bare year number.

4

Eras you can name and add

An era has a name, a short form, whether the short form goes before or after the number, the year it starts, and whether it counts up or down. That covers CE/BCE, the Thai "พ.ศ. 2569", "Third Age 3019", or a count that restarts after a cataclysm.

5

A short setup page, with more when you want it

The page shows the starting point, "now", eras and months. Leap years, the week, seasons and holidays, and moons sit under More options. Their defaults: Sunday to Saturday, the Gregorian leap rule, equinoxes and solstices, New Year's Day, and one moon with a 29.53-day cycle.

6

An inbox for undated threads

Borrowed from Forge Tales. A thread without a date waits beside the calendar until you drag it onto a day, so plotting doesn't require knowing the date first.

7

Month and Year views, and a jump

Pick a month and type a year to go there directly. Year view shows every month at once with thread days marked.

8

Date formats

Choose how dates read across the app: "Wednesday, 25 November 2026", "November 25, 2026", "25 Nov 2026 CE" or "25/11/2026".

For the code

The calendar becomes one object on the project. eraName, year and months move inside it. Threads keep year/monthIndex/day, but year is now a continuous timeline year (year 1 of the first counting-up era is 1; the year before is 0).

calendar: {
  months:   [{ id, name, days }],
  leapMonthId: "m-feb" | null,
  leap:     { kind: "gregorian" | "every" | "none", every: 4 },
  weekdays: ["Sunday", …],          // length = days per week
  weekStart: 0,                      // index into weekdays
  epochWeekday: 1,                   // weekday of year 1, month 1, day 1
  eras:     [{ id, name, short, place: "before" | "after", start: 1, counts: "up" | "down" }],
  now:      { year: 2026, monthIndex: 10, day: 25 },
  holidays: [{ id, name, monthIndex, day, kind: "holiday" | "season" }],
  moons:    [{ id, name, cycle: 29.53, newMoon: { year, monthIndex, day } }],
  format:   "long" | "us" | "short-era" | "numeric",
}
ThreadEvent: { …, year?: number, monthIndex?: number, day?: number }  // no date = inbox
A

Migration

normalize.ts turns an old project's eraName/year/months into a calendar with one counting-up era named after eraName, no weekdays shown, no leap years, and "now" on day 1 of the first month of year. Existing stories, Lantern and Tide included, keep their own months and era as a custom calendar. New books get Gregorian.

B

One date helper

A small lib/calendar.ts holds daysInYear, dayNumber, weekday, formatDate and moonPhase. The Calendar, Threads, Home timeline, Storybook and AI prompts all format dates through it, so a change in setup shows everywhere. The mockup's script is a working draft of it.

C

Obsidian vault

Calendar.md gains the eras, weekdays, leap rule and holidays in its frontmatter, and threads write their date in the chosen format plus a sortable timeline key.
